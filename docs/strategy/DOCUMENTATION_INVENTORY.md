## Classification Definitions

- AUTHORITATIVE: current controlling source for a defined area.
- SUPPORTING: useful and generally current but not controlling.
- NEEDS REVISION: important but outdated, incomplete, or partially conflicting.
- HISTORICAL: valuable record of earlier decisions, completed phases, or product evolution, including retained audit evidence.
- SUPERSEDED: replaced by newer direction but retained until archival cleanup.
- DUPLICATE: substantially repeats another document without meaningful unique content.
- TEMPORARY: short-lived working document that does not need durable audit retention.
- UNREVIEWABLE: content could not be reliably inspected.

## Authority Hierarchy

1. Repository-wide product strategy
2. Approved architectural decisions
3. Narrow architectural, governance, and UX authorities
4. Active supporting documents
5. Implementation-slice briefs and acceptance criteria
6. Audit evidence
7. Historical reference
8. Superseded and temporary material

Narrow authority cannot override repository-wide product strategy. Titles such as "master," "final," or "source of truth" do not establish authority. Completed phase documents preserve evidence but do not control future direction. Where two documents conflict, the higher authority controls unless a later approved decision explicitly supersedes it.
